# Shopping Research Methodology

Use for substantive product selection, purchase planning, and comparison. It is methodology, not a preselected product list or a substitute for the buyer's actual priorities.

## 1. Define the decision
Establish intended use, must-haves, constraints, budget, location, compatibility, purchase timing, existing alternatives and acceptable tradeoffs. Ask only the questions whose answers materially affect the shortlist. Include **keep current, repair or buy nothing** when credible.

## 2. Discover without anchoring
Search relevant categories, brands and sales channels widely before narrowing. Include credible value and lesser-known options, not just search rank, ads or retailers with affiliate links. Confirm the **exact** model number, configuration, region, capacity, size and variant so prices/specifications aren't accidentally mixed.

## 3. Verify evidence
Use manufacturer manuals and specifications for technical claims; verify price, stock, shipping, warranty and returns from current seller/product pages. Assess reliability, repairability, service availability, consumables and long-term satisfaction through independent reporting and user experiences. Separate verified facts, reviewer experience and inference. Surface contradictory sources and material unknowns.

## 4. Compare total value
Compare true landed/ownership cost, including taxes where relevant, delivery, accessories, subscriptions, consumables, servicing, replacement parts, switching friction, energy use and warranty risk. Keep uncertain promotions or rewards separate from dependable price.

## 5. Recommend and verify
Give a small evidence-grounded shortlist with meaningful differences, exclusions and tradeoffs. Explain the buyer-fit recommendation and what would change it. Verify current price and availability immediately before any time-sensitive decision. Never order or commit without explicit authorization.
